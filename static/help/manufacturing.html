You queue what you want built, and the tab plans the whole queue together — what to buy, what to build, on which character, in what order. The short version: set up once, plan a product, add it to your build, then live on the build page until it is delivered.

0. Set up — once per account

Three numbered steps, and only the first is required: where you build. A structure's rigs change the materials and time of every job, so this drives every cost and duration in the tab. The generic presets are real answers — nobody needs a structure configured to get a true plan. Connecting a character adds its real slots, skills and blueprints; leaving the build system blank only means job fees come out light. Save & continue is never disabled.

1. Open the tab

Nothing queued, you get one button: Plan a new build. Something queued, you get the build page — if it is cooking, that is what you came to look at. Running jobs, owned blueprints and asset stock refresh themselves if stale, after the page paints. A stale cache is silently wrong (overstated free slots, buying what is already in your hangar), which is why remembering is not left to you.

2. Plan a product

A preview — nothing is committed. Pick the product and quantity, optionally a label for who it is for, and the containers this build draws from so the plan stops asking you to buy what you already have. The options that shape every number: the facility, whether to ignore small savings, whether to prioritise speed, the build-vs-buy threshold, and your margin. Dragging the threshold shows live what it costs in ISK and saves in time.

These options are saved to your account, not the browser — anything that plans on your behalf without a browser open, like a customer's share link, uses them too.

3. Add it to your build

Queuing re-plans the whole queue together, and that is the reason to queue rather than calculate: components shared between two orders are built in one batch, so the second capital in the queue is cheaper than the first.

4-7. Gather, install, watch

The build page is where you live. It tells you what to acquire, what to install and when, and tracks the jobs as they cook. Material and job progress are read from ESI where it can see them, with manual marks for what it cannot.

8-9. Quote, deliver, clear

A build can be quoted and shared with a customer as a status link they can open without an account — it shows progress, never your other work. When it is delivered, clearing the order takes it out of the queue and the remaining orders re-plan without it.

One thing to know about the numbers

Prices move. A plan is costed when you ask for it, so a quote you gave yesterday is not a quote you can honour today without re-checking. What you are shown is the best answer from the market as it stands, not a promise.
